# Narration-timed animation

Use motion to show concrete entities, actions and relationships in the speaker's
words. A brain inside a dashboard can explain “your brain in a dashboard” better
than a box labeled dashboard. Icons carry meaning; they are not decoration.
Reveal steps as spoken, then hold the final relationship long enough to understand.
Reuse distinctive visuals when an earlier idea returns.

Avoid equally weighted text-box grids and looping arrows for a simple sequence.
Start compact rather than covering the speaker with an empty panel. If crowded,
stage, simplify or reframe instead of shrinking everything. Judge whether the
shapes and movement explain the point before the viewer reads the labels.

Use real source media when it is the subject. Generated art can provide layered
illustrations, not substitute evidence; keep sample data distinct from live values.
Use 2D, 2.5D or 3D according to what depth explains, retaining faithful likenesses
and official marks. Choose engine-specific tools for mechanics rather than adding
an animation pipeline for its own sake.

Inspect scrolls in motion. Slowing duplicate frames or sparse captures can still
stutter; rebuild smooth movement or interpolate only where text stays undistorted.

After narration cuts, map animation time and source-media offsets through the edit.
Splitting an insert should not restart its build or playback unless intentional.
Recheck reveal order, reading holds and payoff against revised speech; restage or
shorten the explanation rather than truncating its conclusion. Preview with actual
dialogue and SFX; still frames cannot verify these timing relationships.
