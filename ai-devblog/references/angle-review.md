# Devblog angle review

Use this gate before drafting every substantial post. Treat a supplied angle
as one candidate unless the user explicitly says to skip review.

## Build the slate

Reconstruct the evidence first, then present 3-5 materially different story
packages. Do not produce cosmetic title variants that share the same thesis.
Vary the actual narrative lens when the evidence permits it: concrete change,
incident, measured result, technical argument, migration, or reusable method.

For each package include:

- proposed title and optional subtitle;
- one-sentence thesis;
- primary reader and the question or promise answered;
- opening artifact or scene;
- strongest visual treatment;
- strongest supporting evidence;
- pros;
- cons, factual caveats, or audience limitations.

Recommend one package. Explain why it best combines truth, reader value,
specificity, and storytelling. Use the `align-me` response shape: lettered
options, concrete tradeoffs, a recommended default, and an explicit approval
shortcut. Wait for the user's choice before drafting unless they explicitly
waive the review.

## Prefer concrete titles

Make the headline name what happened at the same scale as the article. A reader
should be able to distinguish a refactor, incident, benchmark, migration, or
product change without reading the subtitle.

Reject titles that:

- replace a large concrete change with an abstract architectural maxim;
- lead with an internal noun the intended reader cannot place;
- describe an inventory rather than a change or consequence;
- imply a broader result than the evidence supports;
- hide the real event behind a clever hook;
- contradict the causal story or scope discovered during drafting.

Use the subtitle for the broader principle, mechanism, caveat, or secondary
context. Keep a principle-led headline only when the post directly tests or
establishes that principle rather than merely illustrating it.

## Recheck after drafting

Run a second angle audit before publication:

1. Summarize the finished post in one concrete sentence without looking at the
   title.
2. Compare that sentence with the title, subtitle, opening, and primary visual.
3. Confirm that all four promise the same story at the same level of scope.
4. Generate 2-3 replacement title/subtitle pairs when the draft discovered a
   better story or the current title became abstract, overstated, or generic.
5. Ask for another choice when the replacements materially change the angle;
   otherwise use the previously approved framing.

Record the approved package privately so later prose and visual iteration do
not drift into a different article without an explicit decision.
