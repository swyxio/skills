# Reader and angle reference

Use this checkpoint before drafting any post, including a short note. If the
reader, prior knowledge, or desired outcome is open, suggest a reader profile
and ask the user to confirm it. Use choices the user has already made.

## Reader menu

Tailor the choices to the material and ask the open ones in one compact batch.
Use this shape when no better project-specific wording exists:

1. **Reader — I believe the primary reader is:**
   - A. A smart adjacent engineer — explain the project and its minimum model.
   - B. A practitioner in this domain — move faster and emphasize decisions.
   - C. A specialist contributor — prioritize internals and precise tradeoffs.
   - D. A technical decision-maker — prioritize consequences and alternatives.
2. **Prior knowledge — I plan to assume:**
   - A. General software knowledge, but no project-specific nouns.
   - B. Familiarity with the domain, but not this system.
   - C. Familiarity with the named system or ecosystem.
3. **Desired result — I will optimize for the reader to:**
   - A. Gain a useful mental model.
   - B. Make a technical or product decision.
   - C. Try a technique or reproduce a result.
   - D. Understand what happened and why.

Mark a recommendation for each and state its effect. Default to `1A, 2B`, then
choose 3A–3D from the story. End with:

> Reply **approve all** to accept 1A, 2B, 3A, or give changes such as 2C, 3B.

For a short note with an obvious readership, state the inferred answers and
proceed.

## Before, after, and angle

Before drafting, identify the reader's likely starting belief, the evidence-led
belief or action the post should enable, and facts to leave for another piece.
If the angle is open, recommend one and wait for the user’s choice before
drafting, even if only one angle fits. When several angles fit, give each
a distinct title, promise, and tradeoff. See [story modes](story-modes.md).

After drafting, compare the actual subject and conclusion with the title, deck,
opening, primary visual, and evidence. If the premise changes, propagate it
through the section order, limitations, and ending instead of merely replacing
the headline. Preserve any explicit reader or angle choices the user made.
