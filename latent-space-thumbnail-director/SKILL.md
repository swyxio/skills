# Latent Space Thumbnail Director

Use the built-in `$imagegen` tool for **every new or revised thumbnail**, including first looks, quote options, review-board previews and A/B variants. Generate each direction with a separate prompt, using the guest’s real photo and approved branding as references. Start each new angle workshop in text. As soon as you show artwork, show an actual ImageGen result. Never substitute a scripted photo-and-text layout, template render or contact sheet of text swaps. If ImageGen is unavailable, report the blocker and follow its skill’s fallback policy. Code may resize, compress, or arrange finished artwork for review.

Read [preferences](references/preferences.md); consult [resources](references/resource-index.md) for assets and [examples](references/example-index.md) for relevant approved work. Apply the FDE template only when that series is requested.

For a thumbnail workshop, start with the words a viewer would use to name the topic. Read the guest’s cited post or the relevant exchange before writing hooks. Try the plain topic, a specific audience or use case, and a recognizable company or product when each offers a distinct reason to watch. At channel-grid size, can a stranger tell what this episode covers from the artwork alone? Drop any line that needs the title or a spoken explanation to make sense.

Before recommending a headline, check the edited chapters or transcript. Estimate how long the guests develop the idea and where they return to it. If a vivid demo occupies one short stretch, use it as supporting artwork or a chapter hook. Lead with a question the interview spends time answering. When a guest’s earlier work lends authority to a current topic, pair their name with a short, verified “known for” credit.

If swyx asks for thumbnails only, workshop the words on the image and keep the approved video title fixed across variants. If swyx supplies the words and order, carry them verbatim into the artwork brief. See the Resend workshop in [examples](references/example-index.md).

1. Ground ideas in the episode. Review several recent thumbnails on the target channel or series, and compare their composition, type, scale and imagery; distinguish observed patterns from a rigid template.
2. Workshop new thumbnail angles with swyx before generating artwork. Offer at least four distinct title-and-hook pairs unless swyx specifies a different count. For each, name the episode moment that earns the hook, sketch what a viewer would see in one sentence, and give the strongest reason to pass. Recommend one. Use specific sentence-case hooks and verify claims, numbers and roles. Invite swyx to pick, combine, rewrite or reject the directions, then refine them from that feedback. Start ImageGen only when swyx selects an angle for artwork or explicitly delegates the thumbnail angle decision. A request to package, upload, or work in parallel authorizes those tasks; it does not delegate the thumbnail angle. Bring the workshop to the user while independent draft work proceeds. Reuse an already selected angle on resume.
3. For the chosen hook, show distinct visual directions in context alongside recent channel examples, with their tradeoffs. A choice of title or words is not a choice of artwork: obtain visual selection before applying it unless the user explicitly asks you to choose. Independent packaging and private preparation can continue.
4. Build for the episode: one clear idea and a meaningful visual, not generic decorative tech. Show each requested guest and company once, with no unrequested host. Prefer real photo or video-frame cutouts for faces; disclose generated likenesses and inspect fidelity. Use official brand assets, not recreated logos. FDE branding is for FDE episodes only.
5. Inspect full size and at about 320 × 180, including against the channel grid. Check legibility, resemblance, headline, company and guest coverage, contrast, branding, and clearance for YouTube’s bottom-right duration badge. Repair defects; export a 16:9 master and 1280 × 720 JPG.
