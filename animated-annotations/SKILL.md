# Animated annotations

Make the visual answer a listener's question. Verify the point and timing in the actual cut; reveal the entities and their relationships as the speaker explains them, with time for the payoff. Reuse visual callbacks where helpful. A few legible icons may explain a process better than an intricate scene.

## Make the explanation visual

Identify the concrete things, actions and relationships in the speaker's actual words before choosing a layout. Give each important entity a recognizable visual form, and use movement, connection or transformation to show what happens. Use the speaker's own metaphor when it clarifies the idea: for example, a brain inside a dashboard can make “your brain in a dashboard” more immediate than a box labeled “dashboard.” Icons should carry meaning, not merely decorate labels; omit them when the real footage or a simpler treatment communicates better.

Make the progression easy to follow at a glance. Avoid a grid of equally weighted text boxes or a looping return arrow when the idea is a simple sequence; use a clear path, distinct visual roles and concise labels, revealing each step as it is spoken. Let the final relationship or payoff settle. While the diagram builds, avoid covering the speaker with a mostly empty panel: start compact or use the space meaningfully. If a layout becomes crowded, simplify, stage it, or change the framing instead of shrinking the entire diagram. A connected-entities diagram with identifiable subjects may work better than text boxes; do not force every idea into the same diagram or substitute illustration for a requested real source.

Critique the encoded composition in context: ask what the viewer learns from the shapes and motion before reading, whether the path is unambiguous, and whether the key visual is recognizable at viewing size. Compare alternatives against the spoken point, including a working example and a less suitable one when available. A still mockup can explain a proposal but cannot verify animation, timing, audio or playback.

Show the real product, source, chart or recording when it is the subject. Start demos at the payoff; focus pages on the relevant passage and hold it long enough to read. Crop around the useful part instead of shrinking the entire page. Keep example prices and data distinguishable from live values. Generated art is an illustration, not evidence; use real marks and faithful likenesses. Layer and animate generated art in 3D or 2.5D when depth helps; choose simpler 2D when it does not.

Use size, contrast, padding and focus to make each step readable on mobile. Protect the active speaker's eyes and mouth through camera changes; covering listeners is acceptable. Pace reveals to the narration. Scrolls made from sparse captures or slowed duplicate frames can still stutter: inspect the motion and rebuild or interpolate as needed without distorting text.

For animation work, the user authorizes downloading and installing Blender and relevant add-ons when useful. Consider [Frame By Plane](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/frame-by-plane/) for layered art and 2.5D, and [Easeit](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/easeit/) for keyframe easing; check compatibility and use only what the project needs. `annotated-video-production` coordinates episode decisions and owns sound-effects guidance; use its music and SFX direction for animation cues. Route engine-specific HyperFrames work through `hyperframes` and its relevant skills.

When narration is cut or retimed, map animation time and source-media offsets through the edit. Splitting an insert across retained segments should not restart its build or source playback unless that restart is intentional. Recheck the reveal order, reading holds and final payoff against the revised speech; shorten or restage the explanation if needed instead of truncating its conclusion. Keep callbacks consistent with the earlier explanation.

## Review and handoff

Offer a short playable preview with the conversation audio when useful, without adding an approval gate to authorized work. If motion or sound playback is unavailable, disclose that before a full render and provide the preview for review; continue authorized work without claiming playback was checked. Distinguish recordings, animations, stills and proposals; never call a static screenshot an animation. Preserve accepted versus tentative choices.

Inspect the encoded insert in motion and with audio, including the payoff, transitions and relevant camera changes; check readability at a small size. Verify that the intended visual actually appears and is understandable. Sampled frames alone cannot establish motion or sound quality: report playback limits and do not claim checks that were not performed. An audit ends with findings; fix defects only when authorized. A local render is not a published update.
